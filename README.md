# Is TikTok Safe? Privacy and Safety Guide for 2026

TikTok is bigger than ever. Its global monthly active users passed **2 billion for the first time in Q1 2026**, according to Sensor Tower data, and roughly **200 million Americans** use the app, which has been run in the US by a new US-owned joint venture since January 2026.

But bigger doesn't mean safer. In January 2026, TikTok's updated US privacy policy sparked a wave of user anxiety after people noticed it lists sensitive information like **"citizenship or immigration status"** and says the app can collect **precise location** if users grant permission, according to [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/27/tiktok-privacy-policy-immigration-status-ice-protests-user-anxiety-big-tech-social-media/). Scammers are also using the platform: in 2025, security researchers found TikTok videos tricking viewers into installing **information-stealing malware**, with one video reaching nearly **500,000 views**, as reported by [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/05/hackers-use-tiktok-videos-to-distribute.html).

So, is TikTok safe? The honest answer is: it's reasonably safe for most adults who use strong privacy settings and avoid scams, but it collects a lot of data and carries real risks, especially for kids. In this guide, I'll explain what changed in 2026, what TikTok collects, how TikTok malware scams work and exactly how to stay safe.

## Is TikTok Safe to Use in 2026?

**TikTok is generally safe to use for adults who lock down their privacy settings, avoid suspicious links and don't share sensitive information, but it collects a large amount of personal data and has real risks around scams, privacy and younger users.**

Quick summary:

- **For most adults:** Reasonably safe with strong privacy settings
- **For kids and teens:** Use with parental controls and close supervision
- **For privacy-focused users:** Be aware that TikTok collects a lot of data, like most social apps
- **For everyone:** Watch out for scams, fake giveaways and malware tricks

## What Changed for TikTok in the US in 2026?

**In January 2026, TikTok's US operations moved to a new US-owned joint venture, and the app updated its US privacy policy and terms of service.**

Key changes and concerns:

- **New ownership:** TikTok in the US is now run by a US-owned joint venture, created to address national security concerns about data and Chinese ownership
- **Updated privacy policy:** Users received an in-app notice about new privacy terms
- **Sensitive information wording:** The policy lists "citizenship or immigration status" among sensitive information it may process. Experts noted similar wording appeared in earlier versions, but the timing caused new alarm
- **Location data:** The new policy says TikTok can collect approximate or precise location if you give permission, while a 2024 version said the US app didn't collect GPS location
- **User backlash:** Some users called for boycotts and deleted the app

**What this means for you:** Check your location permissions, review what you share and read new privacy notices instead of skipping them.

## What Data Does TikTok Collect?

**TikTok collects account details, content you post, how you use the app, device information and, with permission, contacts and location.**

Common types of data TikTok collects:

- **Profile information:** Name, username, age, email or phone number
- **Content:** Videos, comments, messages and drafts
- **Usage data:** What you watch, like, share and search
- **Device data:** Phone model, operating system, IP address and app activity
- **Optional data:** Contacts, location and other permissions you grant

This is similar to other big social apps, but TikTok's watch-time data is very detailed, because its algorithm learns from every video you watch.

## Is TikTok Safe for Kids and Teens?

**TikTok can be safer for teens with its built-in teen protections and Family Pairing, but parents should still supervise use closely.**

TikTok's teen protections include:

- **Private accounts by default** for younger teens
- **Limits on direct messages** for younger users
- **Default daily screen time limits** for users under 18
- **Family Pairing**, which lets parents link accounts to manage screen time, content filters and privacy

Risks for younger users include contact from strangers, harmful trends and challenges, inappropriate content and too much screen time. TikTok requires users to be at least 13.

## What Is TikTok Malware and How Do Scams Work?

**TikTok malware scams use videos, comments or fake links to trick users into downloading harmful software or giving away personal information.**

Scammers go where the audience is, and with [TikTok monthly active users](https://bloggervoice.com/how-many-people-use-tiktok/) now above 2 billion, the app is one of the biggest targets online.

The biggest recent example:

- **Fake "free software" videos:** In 2025, Trend Micro researchers found TikTok videos, likely made with AI, that told viewers how to "activate" Windows, Microsoft Office, CapCut or Spotify for free
- **The trick:** Videos asked viewers to press Windows + R, open PowerShell and run a command shown on screen
- **The result:** That command installed **Vidar** and **StealC**, malware that steals passwords, cookies and other data

Other common TikTok scams:

- **Fake giveaways** asking you to click a link or pay a "fee"
- **Fake brand or influencer accounts** sending DMs
- **Phishing links** that copy TikTok's login page
- **Fake TikTok apps** or "TikTok Plus" downloads outside official app stores
- **Investment and crypto scams** promising quick profits

**Golden rule:** Never run commands, download software or enter passwords because a video or DM told you to.

## How Do You Make TikTok Safer? (15 Settings and Tips)

**Make your account private, limit who can contact you, turn off unnecessary permissions, enable two-step verification and avoid suspicious links.**

### Privacy Settings

1. **Make your account private** in Settings and privacy, then Privacy
2. **Turn off "Suggest your account to others"**
3. **Limit who can comment, mention, Duet and Stitch** with you
4. **Restrict direct messages** to friends or no one
5. **Turn off contact syncing** and Facebook friend suggestions
6. **Review location permissions** on your phone and turn off precise location

### Security Settings

7. **Turn on two-step verification**
8. **Use a strong, unique password**
9. **Check logged-in devices** and remove ones you don't recognize
10. **Only download TikTok** from the official App Store or Google Play

### Smart Habits

11. **Never share personal details**, like your address, school or workplace
12. **Don't click unknown links** in comments, bios or DMs
13. **Never run commands or install software** from a TikTok video
14. **Use screen time limits** to manage your time on the app
15. **Report and block** suspicious accounts

## Should You Delete TikTok?

**You don't need to delete TikTok if you use strong privacy settings and avoid scams, but deleting it is reasonable if you're uncomfortable with how much data it collects.**

Consider deleting or pausing TikTok if:

- **You're worried** about the 2026 privacy policy changes
- **You work in a sensitive job**, like government or defense, where some employers restrict TikTok
- **The app is hurting** your sleep, focus or mental health

## How Do You Delete Your TikTok Data?

**Go to Settings and privacy, then Account, to download your data or deactivate or delete your account.**

1. **Download your data** first if you want a copy
2. **Choose Deactivate or delete account**
3. **Follow the steps** to confirm
4. **Revoke app permissions** on your phone after deleting

TikTok usually gives you a short period to reactivate before your account is permanently deleted.
